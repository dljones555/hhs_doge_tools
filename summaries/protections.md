# Whistleblower Protections — Federal, California, Orange County

## Context
Concerned citizen in Orange County, CA analyzing public CMS Medicaid Provider
Spending data. Some flagged entities operate in the local behavioral health
service ecosystem. Reliance on nearby public services creates proximity concerns.

---

## FEDERAL

- **False Claims Act 31 U.S.C. 3730(h)** — anti-retaliation covers "any employee, contractor, or agent" but also extends to **associated others**. Retaliation against a qui tam relator is a separate federal cause of action with damages
- **42 U.S.C. 1983** — if any government-funded entity retaliates against you for exercising First Amendment rights (petitioning the government with a fraud complaint), that's a civil rights violation
- **Medicaid Patient/Beneficiary protections** — if you receive Medicaid services, retaliating against a beneficiary for reporting fraud violates federal Medicaid regulations. Providers can lose their Medicaid enrollment for this

## CALIFORNIA

- **Government Code 12653(b)** — California False Claims Act anti-retaliation: "no person may discharge, demean, suspend, threaten, harass, deny promotion to, or in any other manner discriminate against" a qui tam plaintiff
- **Labor Code 1102.5** — whistleblower protection applies broadly, not just to employees
- **Welfare & Institutions Code 10850.4** — protects Medi-Cal beneficiaries who report fraud from service denial or retaliation
- **Penal Code 136.1** — intimidating or threatening a witness/complainant in a fraud matter is a criminal offense

## ORANGE COUNTY SPECIFIC

- **OC Medi-Cal Managed Care** — if you receive services through CalOptima (Orange County's Medi-Cal managed care plan), they have a dedicated compliance hotline and are federally required to protect beneficiaries who report fraud
- **CA DHCS MFCU** (Medicaid Fraud Control Unit, under the AG's office) handles Orange County investigations and has victim/witness protection coordination

## PRACTICAL PROTECTIONS

1. **File under seal through an attorney** — nobody in your community knows you filed. The seal typically lasts months to years
2. **You are using public data** — you have no insider access to disclose. There is nothing to trace back to you unless you tell someone
3. **Document any harassment you're currently experiencing** — dates, locations, individuals. If it escalates after a filing, it becomes evidence of retaliation even if they don't know about the filing specifically
4. **If you depend on behavioral health services from any of these entities**, your attorney can flag this to DOJ — they can ensure your care continuity is protected as part of the investigation
5. **Good faith complaint immunity** — California Civil Code 47(b) provides absolute privilege for communications made in connection with judicial or quasi-judicial proceedings, including fraud complaints to government agencies

## IMMEDIATE SAFETY

If you feel physically unsafe at any point, that's a separate and immediate matter:
- **911** for emergencies
- **OC District Attorney's Victim-Witness Assistance**: (714) 834-3600

## KEY PRINCIPLE

Your good faith analysis of public federal data is protected speech and protected
civic activity at every level of government. The entities you're flagging receive
public money — accountability for that spending is a public right.
